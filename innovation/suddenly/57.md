Method #16 and Method #17 from the TRIZ methodology both involve leveraging physical phenomena to inspire innovative solutions in different fields, including software systems. However, they differ in the underlying principles and the way they can be applied to solve problems.

**Method #16: Effects of the Corona Discharge**

- **Physical Phenomenon**: The corona discharge occurs when the electric field around a conductor becomes intense enough to ionize the surrounding air, leading to a discharge that can prevent larger electrical breakdowns.

- **Application in Software Systems**: In software, this method inspires solutions where the system proactively manages excess stress or load before it leads to failure. It focuses on mitigating issues as they begin to arise by dispersing or handling excess capacity.

  - **Example**: Implementing autoscaling in cloud services, where the system detects increased demand and automatically adds resources to handle the load, preventing system overload.

- **Emphasis**: Preventative actions to handle excess and maintain system stability by addressing issues promptly.

**Method #17: Curie Point of Ferromagnetic Materials**

- **Physical Phenomenon**: The Curie point is the temperature at which a ferromagnetic material loses its permanent magnetic properties and becomes paramagnetic due to thermal agitation disrupting the alignment of its magnetic domains.

- **Application in Software Systems**: This method inspires systems that undergo a fundamental change in behavior or state when a critical threshold or condition is met. It emphasizes adaptability and transformation in response to environmental changes.

  - **Example**: A software application that shifts from a normal operation mode to a degraded or safe mode when critical system parameters (like temperature, memory usage, or battery level) reach a specific threshold. For instance, a smartphone entering power-saving mode when the battery is low.

- **Emphasis**: Transformative actions that change the system's properties or behavior to adapt to new conditions.

**Differences Between Method #16 and Method #17**

1. **Nature of the Response**:
   - *Method #16* focuses on **preventing a problem** by managing excess before it causes failure. It deals with dispersing or mitigating the excess to maintain normal operations.
   - *Method #17* involves a **fundamental change** in the system's state or properties when a critical point is reached. It adapts by altering its behavior to suit new conditions.

2. **Type of Change**:
   - *Method #16* encourages **gradual adjustments** to handle increasing stress, preventing catastrophic failure.
   - *Method #17* prompts a **sudden transformation** in response to crossing a critical threshold, leading to a different mode of operation.

3. **System Adaptability**:
   - *Method #16* is about maintaining **stability** through proactive measures.
   - *Method #17* is about **adaptability and flexibility**, allowing the system to continue functioning under new conditions by changing its fundamental characteristics.

**Illustrative Examples in Software Systems**

- **Method #16 Example**:
  - A web application experiences a surge in user traffic. To prevent server overload, it automatically distributes the load across additional servers (autoscaling) or reduces the quality of non-essential services. This preempts potential failures by handling the "excess energy" in the system.

- **Method #17 Example**:
  - A database system detects that it's reaching storage capacity limits. Upon hitting a critical threshold, it switches to a different data compression algorithm or moves old data to archive storage, fundamentally changing how it manages data to adapt to the new condition.

**Summary**

While both methods address how systems respond to critical conditions, Method #16 is about **managing excess to prevent failure**, and Method #17 is about **altering system behavior to adapt to new conditions**. Applying these concepts to software systems encourages innovative solutions that enhance performance, reliability, and adaptability by drawing inspiration from physical phenomena.
